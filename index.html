  • You own a home in Virginia and want to understand how that affects probate and transfer planning.

What you get in your estate planning process

Your recommendations depend on your goals, assets, and family situation. Common planning deliverables can include:

Last Will and Testament

Directions for distribution, fiduciary appointments, and key instructions aligned with your priorities.

Revocable Living Trust (if appropriate)

Used in situations where trust-based planning may support privacy, management continuity, or probate efficiency.

Durable Power of Attorney

Authorization for trusted decision-makers to handle financial/legal matters if you are unable to act.

Advance Medical Directive

Documents healthcare instructions and appoints someone to advocate for your medical decisions.

Beneficiary Designation Review

A practical check of retirement accounts and insurance beneficiary designations so your overall plan works together.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a trust?

Not always. Some Virginia families benefit from trust planning, while others may be well served with a will-centered plan. The right fit depends on your goals, assets, and household needs.

What does it cost?

Fees vary based on plan complexity and document scope. During consultation, you can discuss your priorities and receive clear information about options and pricing.

How long does the process take?

Timelines differ by responsiveness and complexity, but many plans can move from consultation to signing within a few weeks.

What if I have minor kids?

Planning often includes naming guardians and addressing how assets can be managed for children in practical, age-appropriate ways.

What if I own a home in Virginia?

Home ownership can affect transfer planning and probate strategy. Your plan should account for your deed, ownership structure, and broader goals.

What if I already have estate planning documents?

A review can identify what still works, what may need updates, and how to better align older documents with your current life and objectives.

Do you offer virtual/Zoom consultations?

Yes, many clients prefer virtual meetings for convenience. In-person availability depends on scheduling and location preferences.

What happens after signing?

You receive guidance on next steps, including storing originals, sharing key information with trusted people, and identifying when updates are wise after major life events.
